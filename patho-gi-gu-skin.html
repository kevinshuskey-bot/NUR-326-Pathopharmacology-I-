GI, GU, Skin & Hypersensitivity

Laxative/antidiarrheal logic, UTI pharmacology, the four hypersensitivity types (and where EpiPen fits), antifungals, common skin infections, and stress/steroid physiology.

GU: UTIs & overactive bladder

E. coli is the most common UTI organism; more common in females (short urethra). Bacteriuria = bacteria without infection; urethritis vs. cystitis distinguish infection site.

First-line pharmacology

Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (Bactrim) is first-line (avoid with sulfa allergy → use ciprofloxacin instead). Nitrofurantoin for recurring lower UTIs. Phenazopyridine (Pyridium) is a urinary analgesic, not an antibiotic — causes reddish-orange urine, harmless.

Mirabegron (Myrbetriq): beta-3 agonist, relaxes bladder smooth muscle for overactive bladder — watch for hypertension. Anticholinergics (overactive bladder/incontinence): block acetylcholine → dry mouth, constipation.

Hypersensitivity reactions

TypeMechanismExamples
I — IgE-mediatedMast cell degranulationAllergic rhinitis, asthma, hives, anaphylaxis
II — CytotoxicIgG/IgM antibodies attack cells directlyTransfusion reaction, Rh incompatibility, hemolytic anemia, myasthenia gravis
III — Immune complexAntigen-antibody complexes deposit in tissue → inflammation destroys itRheumatoid arthritis, lupus, glomerulonephritis
IV — Delayed (T-cell, no antibody)T-cell activation, no B-cells involvedPoison ivy, TB skin test, contact dermatitis (peaks 48–72 hrs)
Epinephrine for anaphylaxis

Halts mediator release from mast cells. All routes except PO; SQ preferred. Adult dose 0.2–1 mg; peds 0.01 mg/kg. Watch for angina, arrhythmias, HTN, tachycardia, tremor. Overdose can be fatal — have a coworker verify the dose.

Drug/classNotes

Skin disorders — quick hits

  • Tinea (dermatophyte) infections: pedis (athlete's foot), capitis (scalp — needs PO antifungal, topicals don't work), versicolor (yeast, discolored patches, treat with topical/shampoo antifungal)
  • Herpes zoster (shingles): reactivated VZV along a dermatome; painful vesicles that don't cross midline; treat with antivirals; can leave postherpetic neuralgia
  • Impetigo: honey-colored crusted lesions; treat with topical antibacterial (Bactroban)
  • Cellulitis: red, warm, painful, spreading skin/tissue infection; PO or IV antibiotics depending on severity
  • MRSA: hospital-acquired treated with IV vancomycin/Zyvox; community-acquired treated with Bactrim/dicloxacillin; prophylaxis with Bactroban nasal ointment pre-surgery
  • Skin cancers: basal cell (most common, least likely to metastasize) < squamous cell (can metastasize) < melanoma (rarer, highest metastasis rate)

Stress & steroid physiology

General Adaptation Syndrome: alarm → resistance → exhaustion. Cortisol raises blood glucose (opposite of insulin), promotes gluconeogenesis, and is catabolic to muscle/lymph/skin while promoting fat redistribution to the face/trunk.

Corticosteroid teaching — the same point every time

Never abruptly stop — must taper (risk of adrenal insufficiency). Long-term use: cataracts, hyperglycemia, infection risk, osteoporosis, thin skin/bruising, adrenal suppression. Monitor weight and blood glucose; medical-alert bracelet for long-term use.

Fludrocortisone (Florinef) is the only mineralocorticoid replacement — treats salt-wasting when glucocorticoid replacement alone isn't enough.
