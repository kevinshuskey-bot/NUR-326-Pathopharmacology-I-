Dementia, Depression & SUD

Telling delirium apart from dementia, matching the antidepressant class to its adverse-effect signature, and what withdrawal actually looks like for the major substance classes.

Delirium vs. dementia

DeliriumDementia
OnsetAcute — often hospitalization-relatedInsidious, progressive
CourseFluctuating, reversibleChronic, slow decline
DurationHours to monthsMonths to years
AttentionImpairedIntact early, impaired late
Associated withUTI, thyroid disorder, hypoxia, hypoglycemiaUsually none
Hyperactive delirium

Restless, agitated

Develops over 2–3 days; risk factors: benzos/narcotics, sepsis, surgery, hypoxia. Can progress to hallucinations.

Hypoactive delirium

Sedated, withdrawn

More common with metabolic disorders (liver/kidney failure); decreased alertness, forgetful, apathetic, frequently falls asleep.

Delirium is often preventable and the goal is identifying and removing the cause. Dementia has a genetic predisposition, no cure — treatment targets function.

Alzheimer's disease

Accumulation of neuritic plaques and neurofibrillary tau tangles in the cerebral cortex/hippocampus — disrupts nerve transmission, kills neurons, and lowers acetylcholine. Changes begin decades before symptoms.

DrugMOAIndication
Donepezil (Aricept)Inhibits acetylcholinesterase → ↑acetylcholineMild–moderate AD; give at bedtime with food
Memantine (Namenda)Blocks NMDA receptor stimulationModerate–severe AD

Depression & anxiety — pharmacology

ClassMOAKey adverse effects
SSRIsInhibit serotonin reuptakeWeight gain, GI upset, sexual dysfunction, serotonin syndrome risk with MAOIs; therapeutic benefit takes 3–4 weeks
SNRIsBlock serotonin + norepinephrine reuptakeNausea, insomnia, sexual dysfunction; contraindicated with MAOIs
TCAsBlock norepi + serotonin reuptakeSedation, orthostatic hypotension, anticholinergic effects, cardiac toxicity — fatal in overdose
MAOIsInhibit MAO enzyme → ↑norepi/serotonin/dopamineHypertensive crisis with tyramine (aged cheese, smoked meats, red wine)
Bupropion (Wellbutrin)Unclear; amphetamine-like structureSeizure risk, agitation, weight loss (vs. SSRI weight gain)
BenzodiazepinesEnhance GABACNS/respiratory depression, memory loss, withdrawal risk; short-term only; antidote flumazenil

SSRIs and SNRIs are first-line for safety. Always assess suicide risk when starting or adjusting antidepressants; monitor 4–8 weeks for efficacy before adjusting.

Anxiety disorder quick reference

Panic disorder: anticipatory + avoidance anxiety; CBT + SSRI/SNRI first-line, benzos second-line. GAD: ≥6 months of excessive worry; buspirone is an option with no sedation/abuse potential. PTSD: hyperarousal, avoidance, re-experiencing; paroxetine/sertraline (SSRI) are FDA-approved. OCD: SSRIs first-line, notoriously difficult to treat.

Substance use disorder & withdrawal

Addiction is a disease driven by dopamine surges that exceed the normal pleasure response and disturb normal reward pathways (receptor downregulation) — tied to prefrontal cortex dysfunction.

SubstanceWithdrawal timeline & pictureDanger
OpioidsBegins 12–24 hrs after last dose, peaks ~72 hrs, lasts 5–10 days; flu-like, cramping, cravings, anxietyMiserable but rarely fatal; treat supportively + MAT
Alcohol / benzodiazepinesBoth act on GABA — similar picture and treatmentCan be fatal — seizures, hallucinations, delirium tremens, Wernicke's encephalopathy (confusion + oculomotor dysfunction + ataxia)
Treatment

Alcohol/benzo withdrawal: benzodiazepine taper to prevent seizures/DTs, plus thiamine (B1) supplementation. Opioid withdrawal: methadone (full mu-agonist MAT) or buprenorphine/naloxone (Suboxone — partial agonist + antagonist, lower abuse potential).
