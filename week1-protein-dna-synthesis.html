Aminoglycosides Through Metronidazole

This group doesn't touch the cell wall at all — instead it jams the ribosome (protein synthesis) or corrupts DNA/folate synthesis directly. Six drug families, one shared idea: get inside the cell and stop it from building what it needs.

Aminoglycosides

gentamicin · amikacin · tobramycin

Potent against gram (−) bacteria; need to be paired with another agent for a synergistic effect against gram (+). In use since 1944 for complicated infections — UTI/pyelonephritis, gynecological infections, peritonitis, endocarditis, pneumonia, diabetic osteomyelitis.

Severe side-effect profile
  • Nephrotoxicity — 5–25% of patients, usually reversible
  • Ototoxicity — 3–14%, usually permanent
  • Requires therapeutic drug monitoring (peak/trough levels); dosing has shifted from 3×/day to once-daily
  • Neuromuscular blockade — can cause profound respiratory distress, especially with myasthenia gravis
  • CNS effects: confusion, depression, disorientation, numbness/tingling
  • Cochlear damage: high-frequency hearing loss, high-pitched tinnitus

Lincosamides

clindamycin

Bactericidal or bacteriostatic depending on concentration. Binds ribosomes to inhibit protein synthesis. Used for chronic bone infections, GU infections, intra-abdominal infections, anaerobic pneumonia, septicemia, serious skin infections, and endocarditis prophylaxis. Available PO and IV.

Watch for

Very toxic — monitor levels; caution with neuromuscular blockers. Can cause deadly pseudomembranous colitis.

Macrolides

erythromycin · azithromycin (Z-Pak)

Bacteriostatic in general, bactericidal at high concentration. Bind ribosomes to inhibit protein synthesis. Cover upper/lower respiratory infections, skin/soft tissue infections, and STIs — also effective against Legionnaires', Listeria, and mycoplasma pneumonia.

Erythromycin

Broad but rough on the gut

Intense GI side effects. Bonus use: increases gastric motility (helps diabetic gastroparesis). Doesn't cross the BBB. IV form is painful; oral absorption is poor — don't give on an empty stomach.

Azithromycin

Better tolerated

Less GI upset, very good tissue penetration, long duration of action. Take without food — food decreases absorption.

Tetracyclines

tetracycline · doxycycline · minocycline

Bacteriostatic — bind ribosomes to inhibit protein synthesis. Broad spectrum historically, though major resistance has developed. Still commonly used for Rocky Mountain spotted fever, chlamydia/trichomonas, Lyme disease, cholera, pelvic inflammatory disease, mycoplasma pneumonia, and acne.

Contraindications & adverse effects

Do not give to pregnant/nursing patients or children under 8 — causes permanent tooth discoloration and enamel hypoplasia. Also: photosensitivity, N/V/D, headache, dizziness; rare anaphylaxis/angioedema. Not available parenterally; fasting improves oral absorption (~75–77%). Concentrates in bone, liver, tumor tissue, spleen, and teeth.

Fluoroquinolones

ciprofloxacin · levofloxacin (Levaquin)

Very potent, broad-spectrum, good oral absorption. MOA: alter bacterial DNA by interfering with DNA gyrase and topoisomerase. Mostly gram (−) coverage with some gram (+). (1st/2nd generation quinolones are no longer used.)

Ciprofloxacin

UTIs, STIs, anthrax

PO, IV, topical. Minimal BBB/CSF penetration. Adverse effect: arthropathy (joint disease), often irreversible — avoid under 18 and over 60.

Levofloxacin

Once-daily dosing

Most widely used quinolone; 100% oral bioavailability, less resistance, good against pneumococcal/"atypical" respiratory infections. Watch for seizure-predisposing CNS effects, kidney failure, QT prolongation, photosensitivity.

Sulfonamides

sulfamethoxazole + trimethoprim (Bactrim)

The only sulfonamide combination still used clinically. MOA: bacteriostatic — prevents synthesis of folic acid needed for DNA synthesis (doesn't directly destroy the organism). Used for uncomplicated UTIs, respiratory infections, salmonella, shigellosis.

Watch for

Sulfa allergy, photosensitivity.

Metronidazole (Flagyl)

Antiprotozoal and antibacterial — anaerobic activity only. MOA: inhibits DNA synthesis (similar concept to fluoroquinolones). Used for Crohn's disease flares and antibiotic-associated diarrhea (including C. diff).

Do not take with alcohol

Other adverse effects: nausea/vomiting, xerostomia (dry mouth), vaginal candidiasis.
