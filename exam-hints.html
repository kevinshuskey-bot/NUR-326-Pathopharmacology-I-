Must Know Quick Reference

The facts most likely to show up on an exam, pulled across all five Week 1 topics. ★ marks the highest-yield items.

Week 1 Content

Antimicrobial Principles

  • ★ Bactericidal = kills the organism. Bacteriostatic = only inhibits growth; the immune system finishes it off.
  • ★ Allergy vs. adverse effect: N/V is an adverse effect, not an allergy. Rash, welts, angioedema, and anaphylaxis are true allergies.
  • ★ Allergic to one penicillin = allergic to all penicillins. Penicillin–cephalosporin cross-reactivity is real but low (1–4%) — avoid cephalosporins entirely if the PCN reaction was anaphylactic.
  • Blood cultures: draw two sets (aerobic + anaerobic), peripheral stick preferred over central line.
  • MIC = lowest concentration that stops growth. MBC = lowest concentration that kills ≥99.9%. Reported clinically as susceptible/resistant.
  • ★ ESKAPE organisms: Enterococcus faecium, Staph aureus, Klebsiella pneumoniae, Acinetobacter baumannii, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Enterobacter spp.
  • C. diff spores survive 5+ months on surfaces.

Beta-Lactams & Vancomycin

  • ★ All beta-lactams (penicillins, cephalosporins, carbapenems, monobactams) inhibit cell wall synthesis. Beta-lactamase-producing bacteria can defeat them — hence the pairing with sulbactam / clavulanic acid / tazobactam / avibactam.
  • ★ Piperacillin-tazobactam (Zosyn) is the extended-spectrum penicillin, always given with a beta-lactamase inhibitor, strong anti-pseudomonal coverage.
  • Cephalosporin generations: higher generation = broader gram − coverage and better CSF penetration. Ceftriaxone (3rd gen) and cefepime (4th gen) cross the BBB.
  • ★ Carbapenems = broadest spectrum of ALL antibiotics; biggest risk is seizures; all IV, infused over 60 minutes.
  • ★ Vancomycin: covers MRSA; oral form treats C. diff (stays in the gut); toxicities = oto/nephrotoxicity; Red Man Syndrome (flushing/rash/hypotension from rapid infusion) is NOT a true allergy — slow the infusion.

Protein / DNA / Folate Synthesis Inhibitors

  • ★ Aminoglycosides (gentamicin): nephrotoxicity (reversible) + ototoxicity (usually permanent); requires peak/trough monitoring.
  • Clindamycin: watch for deadly pseudomembranous colitis.
  • Azithromycin — take without food. Erythromycin — take with food (GI upset) and it boosts gastric motility.
  • ★ Tetracyclines: contraindicated in pregnancy and children < 8 (permanent tooth discoloration); also photosensitivity.
  • ★ Fluoroquinolones (cipro, levofloxacin): risk of tendon/joint damage (arthropathy) — avoid under 18 and over 60.
  • Bactrim (sulfamethoxazole/trimethoprim): sulfa allergy, photosensitivity.
  • ★ Metronidazole (Flagyl): never with alcohol.

Antivirals (non-HIV)

  • ★ Antivirals are limited because the virus is often done replicating by the time symptoms appear, they only work during active replication, and they risk harming host cells.
  • Acyclovir/valacyclovir/famciclovir suppress HSV/VZV — not a cure, disease can recur. IV form: watch for tissue necrosis if it infiltrates.
  • ★ Oseltamivir (Tamiflu): most effective within 48 hours of flu symptom onset; mainly influenza A.
  • Ganciclovir treats CMV — black box warnings for hematologic toxicity, fertility/fetal toxicity, carcinogenesis.

HIV

  • ★ AIDS diagnosis = an AIDS-defining condition OR CD4 count < 200 cells/mm³.
  • Order of the HIV life cycle: bind CD4 → fuse/enter → reverse transcriptase makes DNA copy → integrase inserts it into host DNA → protease assembles new virus.
  • ★ Clinical stages: acute (highly infectious, often asymptomatic) → clinical latency (3–12 yrs untreated, decades treated) → symptomatic/AIDS.
  • ART = typically 2 nucleoside drugs (NRTIs) + 1 drug from another class. Goal: undetectable viral load.
  • ★ NRTI black box warning: lactic acidosis syndrome.
  • ★ PrEP = before exposure, daily pill, >90% risk reduction. PEP = after exposure, 28-day ART course, HIV testing at 6 and 12 weeks.
  • Needle-stick exposure to HIV-positive source: transmission risk is still < 1%.

Not Tested

Things the lecture explicitly said not to memorize.

  • Specific drug names for HIV/ART therapy (generalizations only)
  • Exact MIC/MBC values or concentrations
  • Doses of any antimicrobial
