Cellular Adaptation, Infection & Immunity

How cells adapt (or fail to), how infection actually unfolds step by step, and how the immune system organizes its response.

Cellular adaptation

Changes cells make to survive stress. Most are reversible; neoplasia is not.

Atrophy

Cell shrinks

Decreased workload, nutrition, blood supply, hormones, or immobility. Decreased protein synthesis / increased catabolism.

Hypertrophy

Cell enlarges

Response to mechanical stimuli (stretch, pressure, volume overload). Heart and kidney are prone to it as a negative adaptation.

Hyperplasia

Cell number increases

Only in cells that CAN divide (skin, intestinal epithelium, glandular). Physiologic (pregnancy, wound healing) or pathologic (cancer, abnormal hormone stimulation).

Dysplasia

Abnormal size/shape/organization

Mild/moderate/severe. Associated with chronic irritation — can be reversible. Dysplasia ≠ cancer.

Metaplasia

One mature cell type replaces another

Reversible; a response to chronic irritation that lets cells survive better in a hostile environment — but can predispose to cancer.

Neoplasia

Uncontrolled growth

Loss of differentiation (anaplasia); irreversible. Usually from gene mutation.

Benign vs. malignant

Malignant: undifferentiated/anaplastic, rapid atypical reproduction, metastasizes, no capsule. Benign: differentiated, grows slowly, doesn't metastasize, usually encapsulated.

Necrosis & gangrene

Necrosis is irreversible cell death — swelling, bursting, inflammation. Prolonged ischemia → infarction → can progress to gangrene. Dead tissue is a breeding ground for bacteria.

Gangrene typePresentation
DryBlackened, dry, wrinkled tissue
WetLiquefaction, foul smelling, rapid spread — can become systemic
GasClostridium perfringens; gaseous bubbles in tissue

The infectious process, step by step

  1. Injury — brief vasoconstriction (stop bleeding), then prolonged vasodilation (brings immune cells in; causes warmth/redness/swelling).
  2. Increased permeability — fluid moves from vessels into injured tissue.
  3. Leukocyte immigration — neutrophils (first responders), eosinophils, NK cells, monocytes move into tissue.
  4. Phagocytosis — neutrophils and monocytes recognize, engulf, and destroy the organism.
  5. Exudate forms — fluid + debris that transports leukocytes, dilutes toxins, and delivers nutrients for healing. Types: serous, serosanguinous, purulent, hemorrhagic.
  6. Systemic symptoms if it doesn't stay localized — fever set point rises, which slows some bacteria and boosts neutrophil/macrophage function.

Diagnosing infection

Gram stain vs. culture & sensitivity

Gram stain — back in hours; shows gram +/−, shape, arrangement. Culture & sensitivity — 24–72 hours for full ID and sensitivity pattern.

Blood cultures: 2 sets (aerobic + anaerobic), ideally at least one peripheral stick (lower skin-contamination risk than a line). Urinalysis dipstick: nitrites positive = bacteria converting nitrate; leukocyte esterase positive = WBCs present.

Superinfections

Antimicrobials kill normal flora along with the target pathogen, letting resistant organisms overgrow. C. diff: watery orange diarrhea, cramping; diagnosed by stool PCR for toxin, not culture; contact isolation, soap-and-water hand hygiene (resistant to sanitizer); never give antidiarrheals until C. diff is ruled out. Candidiasis: oral/vaginal overgrowth (thrush) — treated with nystatin (swish/spit or suppository).

Inflammation

A protective response to cell injury: destroys/limits harmful agents and preps tissue for repair. "-itis" = inflammation.

Details
Local signsRedness, swelling, heat, pain, loss of function
CausesExogenous (surgery, trauma, burns) or endogenous (tissue ischemia)
SequenceTissue injury → vasodilation/permeability → leukocyte recruitment (chemotaxis) → phagocytosis
SystemicIL-1, IL-6, TNF-α → fever, ↑ neutrophils, lethargy, muscle catabolism

Adaptive immunity

  • MHC / HLA complexGenes on chromosome 6; proteins on cell surfaces that identify "self." Two classes: MHC I and MHC II.
  • B-lymphocytesHumoral immunity. Memory cells remember prior exposure; plasma cells secrete antibodies.
  • T-lymphocytesCell-mediated immunity.
