Patho Review / Topic 4

Diabetes & Metabolic Syndrome

Insulin deficiency vs. insulin resistance, the four insulin types you'll actually give, the oral agent alphabet soup, and where obesity fits into the picture.

The hormone balancing act

Insulin (beta cells) lowers blood glucose — stimulates uptake/storage of glucose, stimulates the liver to store glycogen. Counter-regulatory hormones that raise glucose: glucagon (alpha cells), epinephrine, glucocorticoids, growth hormone.

What happens without enough insulin

Cells can't use glucose → hyperglycemia → cells switch to fatty acids for fuel → increased lipolysis → free fatty acids → ketone bodies → ketosis/acidosis. Protein catabolism also increases (muscle wasting, more typical in type 1). Glycosuria occurs once glucose exceeds renal reabsorption capacity (~200 mg/dL).

The 3 Ps

Polyphagia (hunger, from cellular starvation) · Polydipsia (thirst, from ↑osmolality) · Polyuria (from osmotic diuresis — also loses K, Na, Cl).

All insulin is given SUBQ (except regular, which can also go IV). Always know the blood sugar before giving any insulin.

Somogyi effect vs. Dawn phenomenon

Somogyi: insulin overdose → nighttime hypoglycemia → counter-regulatory rebound → morning hyperglycemia. Dawn phenomenon: morning hyperglycemia from natural early-morning hormone release (cortisol, GH) — not related to overdose.

Oral & injectable agents

ClassMOAKey points
Sulfonylureas (-ide)Close K-ATP channels in beta cells → stimulate insulin secretionHypoglycemia risk (esp. renal/hepatic dysfunction); avoid in pregnancy
Biguanides (metformin)↓ hepatic glucose production, ↑ muscle glucose uptake — does NOT stimulate insulin releaseFirst-line for T2DM; doesn't cause true hypoglycemia; hold 48 hrs post-IV contrast; avoid in heart/kidney/liver failure or heavy alcohol use
DPP-4 inhibitors (-gliptin)Block breakdown of incretins → ↑insulin, ↓glucagonLow hypoglycemia risk; watch for pancreatitis
GLP-1 agonists (-tide)↑glucose-dependent insulin release, ↓glucagon, slows gastric emptying, suppresses appetiteBlack box: thyroid C-cell tumor risk — avoid with history of medullary thyroid carcinoma/MEN2
SGLT2 inhibitors (-flozin)Block renal glucose reabsorption — glucose spilled in urine↑ risk of UTI and genital fungal infection; cardio- and renal-protective; not for ESRD or type 1
Glucagon — the antidote

For severe hypoglycemia: oral tablets if awake/can swallow; SQ/IV/IM if symptomatic with decreased LOC. MOA: activates hepatic glucagon receptors → glycogenolysis. Recheck fingerstick 15 minutes after giving.

Hypoglycemia (both types): blood sugar < 55–60, rapid onset — pallor, sweating, tachycardia, hunger, tremor, confusion, seizure/coma if untreated.

Metabolic syndrome & obesity pharmacology

Metabolic syndrome — need ≥3

Waist ≥40in (men)/≥35in (women) · triglycerides >150 · HDL <40 (men)/<50 (women) · BP ≥130/85 · fasting glucose ≥110. Treatment: treat each piece individually, but weight loss/exercise/diet/smoking cessation matter most.

Orlistat (Xenical/Alli): OTC; blocks gastric/pancreatic enzymes, reduces fat absorption ~30%. Black box: liver injury. GI side effects (oily spotting, flatulence, fecal incontinence) worsen with high-fat meals. Must take a multivitamin. Takes 3 months to see effect.
