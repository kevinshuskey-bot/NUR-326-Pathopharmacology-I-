Patho Review / Topic 3

Hypertension & Hyperlipidemia

Two conditions that share a final common pathway — vascular damage — and a shared drug logic once you understand RAAS.

RAAS — the core loop

  1. Low BP / low blood volume (sodium) → liver releases angiotensinogen
  2. Low renal perfusion → kidney releases renin → converts angiotensinogen to angiotensin I
  3. Angiotensin I travels to the lungs, converted by ACE to angiotensin II
  4. Angiotensin II is a potent vasoconstrictor and triggers the adrenal glands to release aldosterone (fluid retention)
  5. Net result: nephron retains fluid, BP rises

Other BP regulators: vascular autoregulation (arterioles adjust diameter to keep tissue perfusion constant) and arterial baroreceptors (carotid sinus, aorta, left ventricle — alter HR and vasodilation/constriction).

Primary vs. secondary hypertension

Primary (essential)

No known cause

Most common; complex genetic + environmental + neurohormonal (SNS, RAAS, natriuretic peptides).

Secondary

Known cause

Renal disease, adrenal tumors (pheochromocytoma), drug-induced (OCPs, corticosteroids, cocaine, amphetamines). Treat the underlying cause.

The "silent killer"

Usually asymptomatic — you're looking for end-organ damage: chest pain (heart), headache (brain), visual changes (eyes), extremity weakness (stroke). Long-term: LV hypertrophy, accelerated atherosclerosis, aortic aneurysm, ESRD, stroke/retinopathy, gangrene/claudication. Hypertensive crisis = SBP >180 and/or DBP >120.

Cholesterol & hyperlipidemia

Cholesterol is a phospholipid-bilayer building block and precursor for estrogen, testosterone, vitamin D, cortisol, and bile acids. 25% comes from diet (exogenous); 75% is made by the liver (endogenous, via HMG-CoA reductase).

LabTarget
Total cholesterol< 200 mg/dL
HDLFemale > 55, Male > 45 (higher is better)
LDL< 130 mg/dL
Triglycerides< 160 mg/dL

Atherosclerosis, step by step

Endothelial injury (smoking, HTN, hyperglycemia) → increased permeability → LDL enters and oxidizes in the vessel wall → macrophages engulf lipids (become foam cells) → inflammatory mediators released → lipid core builds → plaque ruptures → platelet aggregation → thrombus. CRP is a marker of the systemic inflammation driving this process.

Lipid-lowering pharmacology

Statins (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors)

MOA: inhibit HMG-CoA reductase → less cholesterol made → liver upregulates LDL receptors → more LDL cleared from blood. Effect is not permanent — must keep taking the drug. Take at night (simvastatin, rosuvastatin especially); takes ~2 weeks to see effect.

Statin adverse effects

Myopathy → rhabdomyolysis (muscle breakdown → acute kidney injury) and hepatotoxicity. Avoid alcohol and other drugs that raise myopathy risk.

Ezetimibe (cholesterol absorption inhibitor) blocks absorption in the jejunum — 2nd-line, often combined with a statin (ezetimibe + simvastatin = Vytorin) for a bigger LDL drop than either alone.
