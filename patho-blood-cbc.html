Blood Cells & CBC Interpretation

What each WBC type is telling you, how to read a differential without getting fooled by percentages, and a quick tour of the major blood cancers.

White blood cells — "Never Let Monkeys Eat Bananas"

CellNormal %Rises with
Neutrophils50–67%Acute bacterial infection, trauma. "Shift to the left" = more immature bands released.
Lymphocytes24–40%Chronic bacterial infection, acute viral infection
Monocytes4–9%Bacterial infections, cancers (phagocytosis)
Eosinophils0–3%Allergic reactions, parasitic infections ("worms, wheezes, weird diseases")
Basophils0–2%Allergic/hypersensitivity, inflammatory reactions
Reading a CBC with diff without getting fooled

The 5 percentages always sum to 100% — but an increased percentage of one type doesn't necessarily mean an increased absolute number. To get the real count: % × total WBC count. Example: WBC 20,000 with 15% lymphocytes = 3,000 lymphocytes/mm³ — the same absolute count as when it was 30,000 × 10% at a different total. Always calculate the absolute number before concluding anything increased or decreased.

WBC total: 5,000–10,000/mm³. Leukocytosis = elevated; leukopenia = low; neutropenia = low neutrophils specifically.

Leukopenia treatment: G-CSF agents

Filgrastim (Neupogen) — recombinant G-CSF, promotes granulocyte proliferation/differentiation. Used for chemo-induced leukopenia, BMT, chronic neutropenia. IV/SQ; AE: bone pain, leukocytosis. Pegfilgrastim (Neulasta) is the long-acting, pegylated version (half-life 17 hrs) — SQ only.

Malignant blood disorders — the quick version

ALL

Acute lymphocytic leukemia

Most common childhood leukemia; sudden onset; blasts may be 60–100% of cells.

AML

Acute myelocytic leukemia

Chiefly an adult disease; sudden, stormy onset.

CLL

Chronic lymphocytic leukemia

Most common leukemia in older adults; insidious onset, often found incidentally.

CML

Chronic myelocytic leukemia

Adults & children; leukocytosis with immature granulocytes.

All leukemic cells are immature, proliferate uncontrolled, and can't function as normal phagocytes — and they crowd out normal RBC/platelet production. Treatment: cytotoxic chemo, stem cell transplant (allogeneic/syngeneic/autologous).

Hodgkin lymphomaNon-Hodgkin lymphoma
HallmarkPainless rubbery lymph node (usually neck); Reed-Sternberg cell on biopsyPainless lymphadenopathy; spreads early to liver/spleen/marrow
TreatmentChemo (ABVD regimen), radiation, stem cell transplantChemo, radiation, rituximab and similar agents
Multiple myeloma

Plasma cell (B-cell) cancer — monoclonal "M protein" drives increased osteoclast activity → bone destruction. Classic picture: bone pain/fractures, hypercalcemia, Bence Jones proteins in urine, elevated creatinine.
