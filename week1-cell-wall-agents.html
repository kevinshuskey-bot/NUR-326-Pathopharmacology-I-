Beta-Lactams & Vancomycin

Penicillins, cephalosporins, carbapenems, and vancomycin all attack the same structure — the bacterial cell wall — which is why their side-effect logic and allergy rules echo across the whole family.

Shared goal: inhibit cell wall synthesis

Every drug on this page works by weakening the bacterial cell wall. Fluid rushes into the now-unsupported cell, the cell swells, and it lyses. It only works on organisms that are actively growing and dividing.

Beta-lactam structure

Penicillins, cephalosporins, carbapenems, and monobactams all share a beta-lactam ring and all inhibit synthesis of the bacterial peptidoglycan wall. If an organism produces beta-lactamase, it can break that ring and defeat these drugs — so beta-lactams are often paired with a beta-lactamase inhibitor: sulbactam, clavulanic acid, tazobactam, or avibactam.

Penicillins

Discovered 1896, rediscovered by Fleming in 1928, mass-produced by 1943 — with resistance already emerging by 1947. MOA: disrupt cell wall synthesis by inhibiting transpeptidases (essential enzymes for wall-building) and activate autolysis. Low toxicity overall; main adverse effects are urticaria, pruritis, and angioedema.

Allergy rule

Rash-to-anaphylaxis: an allergy to one penicillin means an allergy to all penicillins.

Penicillin subclasses — increasing spectrum, left to right
SubclassDrugsRouteKey points
Natural penicillin G, penicillin V IV/IM (PO available) Gram +, gram − cocci, anaerobes, spirochetes. Half-life ~30 min (longer with renal dysfunction). Least toxic of the group. Often paired with aminoglycosides for a synergistic effect. IM form commonly used for STI treatment.
Penicillinase-resistant nafcillin (drug of choice; IV only), cloxacillin / oxacillin (PO) IV / PO Built to resist breakdown by penicillinase — the enzyme staph organisms secrete. Sometimes called "anti-staphylococcal" penicillins. (Methicillin used to be in this group; no longer used clinically.)
Aminopenicillins ampicillin, amoxicillin (± clavulanic acid = Augmentin; ± sulbactam = Unasyn) PO or IV First broad-spectrum penicillins. Diarrhea and rash are common. Amoxicillin has fewer side effects, is PO-only, and is a pediatric mainstay for ENT/GU/skin infections. Ampicillin use is declining due to resistance.
Extended-spectrum piperacillin-tazobactam (Zosyn), ticarcillin IV, always with a beta-lactamase inhibitor Broadest of the penicillins; strong anti-pseudomonal activity. Watch platelet function and renal status. Ticarcillin also carries a sodium-overload risk.

Cephalosporins

Structurally similar to penicillins — same target (penicillin-binding protein), same cell-wall-synthesis inhibition, same autolysis trigger. Generally resistant to beta-lactamase (bacteria instead secrete cephalosporinase against them). Low toxicity; small cross-sensitivity risk with penicillin allergy (avoid entirely if the PCN reaction was anaphylactic). As the generation number goes up, spectrum widens and CSF penetration improves.

Five generations
GenDrugsKey points
1stcefazolin (IV only), cephalexinStrong gram + coverage (staph, non-enterococcal strep). Cefazolin is the classic surgical-prophylaxis drug.
2ndcefuroxime, cefotetanAdds gram − coverage on top of gram +. Cefuroxime does not cover anaerobes.
3rdceftriaxone, ceftazidime, cefotaximeMost potent gram − coverage, weaker gram +. Ceftriaxone is extremely long-acting (once-daily) and crosses the BBB — useful for meningitis — but avoid in liver failure. Ceftazidime covers pseudomonas well.
4thcefepimeVery broad — both gram − and +. Crosses the BBB. Used for UTIs, skin infections, pneumonia.
5thceftaroline; ceftolozane-tazobactam (Zerbaxa, newest)Ceftaroline covers MRSA/MSSA and some VRSA/VISA, but not Enterobacter, pseudomonas, ESBL, or Klebsiella. IV only, renally dosed.

Carbapenems

The broadest-spectrum antibiotics available — bactericidal cell wall inhibitors typically reserved as a last resort. All are IV, all must be infused over 60 minutes.

Biggest adverse effect

Drug-induced seizure activity (uncommon overall, but watch closely in the elderly and anyone on other seizure-lowering-threshold medications).

Most broad

Imipenem/cilastatin

Very resistant to beta-lactamase, crosses BBB/meninges. IV only. Highest seizure watch of the class.

Common workhorse

Meropenem

Slightly less coverage than imipenem but doesn't degrade in the kidneys and carries less seizure risk. Rash and diarrhea are most common side effects.

Once daily

Ertapenem

Narrower spectrum, but only needs once-daily dosing.

Newest

Doripenem

Less seizure activity — but not for pneumonia.

CRE — a public-health emergency

Carbapenem-resistant Enterobacteriaceae is a healthcare-acquired infection with mortality as high as 50% in some studies. KPC (K. pneumoniae carbapenemase) is the most common variant in the US; NDM and VIM are less common but more resistant than KPC.

Vancomycin

A glycopeptide (not a beta-lactam) that binds directly to the bacterial cell wall, producing immediate inhibition of synthesis and cell death. Covers gram + infections including MRSA and penicillin-resistant pneumococcus. Doesn't work for CNS infections. Cleared renally — dose down for renal dysfunction.

Route matters

Oral vancomycin is used specifically to treat C. diff / pseudomembranous colitis (it stays in the gut). Systemic infections need IV.

Toxicities to monitor
  • Ototoxicity with high levels — can be reversible
  • Nephrotoxicity — extra caution combined with other nephrotoxic drugs (aminoglycosides, cyclosporine, IV contrast)
  • Immune-mediated thrombocytopenia
  • Caution with neuromuscular blockers
Red Man Syndrome

Flushing, rash, pruritis, urticaria, tachycardia, hypotension — related to rapid infusion, not a true allergy. Usually not harmful; prevent/treat by infusing more slowly over a longer period. Peak and trough levels are used to monitor dosing.

These drugs ALL work by messing up the cell wall. What differentiates them is spectrum and reach — which one covers gram negatives, which one crosses the blood-brain barrier, which one has a signature side effect (vanc → Red Man Syndrome).Module 1 voiceover
